# 8. Language models

Can a small pretrained language model do what the project's small transformers do: track a hidden state from a
history and act on it? One task, built to be as partially observed as possible while keeping the exact belief and the
exact optimum available.

| experiment | question | result | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Bump maze](bump_maze/) | A MiniGrid maze with a known map and goal, an unknown start cell and heading, and one bit of observation per step (a forward move moved or bumped). Zero-shot: do Qwen2.5-Instruct models from 0.5B to 7B act well, and do they track where they could be? Trained: does a fine-tuned 0.5B model reach the exact optimum, is it more data-efficient than a transformer from scratch, and is the consistent set of states decodable from its layers? (The probe result is corrected by the policy study: a lookup over the optimum's sets, not a set code.) | [report](bump_maze/REPORT.md) | 4 h GPU |
| [Bump goals](bump_goals/) | The same maze with four corner goals, one per episode named in the prompt, where the exact additive ceiling is 0.88 (maze10's 0.96). The fine-tuned 0.5B is exactly optimal under four goals at 40 000 episodes (the models from scratch 0.40–0.47 success); its logits are 0.20 goal × history interaction, concentrated at known states, and its additive part run as a policy recovers 0.14 of the goal-directed return (maze10: 0.81). Post hoc, the exact ceiling rule itself recovers 0.14 online when fitted on the optimum's decisions and about 0.5 refitted on its own episodes. Goal swaps of the decision token's residual work only from layer 20 of 24 (0.98 at 23), history swaps from layer 13; at layer 23 a linear map installs 0.90 of the swap and the mean goal offset nothing. | [report](bump_goals/REPORT.md) | 8 h GPU |
| [Bump policy](bump_policy/) | What function does the fine-tuned single-goal model compute, against the exact optimum, a function of the belief set over 17 285 reachable sets? Not that function: the same action on every history of a set in 0.59 of sets, optimal on 0.65 of the sets the training never visits (below a lookup from membership), its logits not reproducible from the set; the set is not in the state on held-out sets (exact ≤ 0.035; the single-goal study's 0.97 was a by-episode lookup over 247 sets, withdrawn), what is there is whether forward will bump; nothing passes through step boundaries, the prefix is read from raw tokens in the first eight layers. 0 of 10 registered criteria held. | [report](bump_policy/REPORT.md) | 1.5 h GPU |
| [Bump rule](bump_rule/) | Exploratory, no plan: what the reward-trained GRUs do instead. On their own episodes they are the optimum with one decision in seven replaced, each costing one to four moves, three quarters of them fixed errors at a few dozen situations (an extra turn at step 2 or 4; straight past a turn at the same dozen known cells in every seed), the rest history dependence at the same known cell; their deviations are less informative moves, not more, and they localise slower than the optimum. The last 12 tokens reproduce the optimum's own decisions as well as theirs (a deterministic policy is a tree of about 1 900 histories), so memory length does not separate them; they depend less on each past token; no k-means machine over the hidden state reproduces either model. | [report](bump_rule/REPORT.md) | 1 h GPU |

Rerun: `python reproduce.py bump_maze`, `python reproduce.py bump_goals`, `python reproduce.py bump_policy`; results of the whole project: [FINDINGS.md](../../FINDINGS.md).
