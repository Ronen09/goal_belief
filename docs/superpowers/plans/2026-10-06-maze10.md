# A 10 × 10 maze: design options (on hold)

Status: on hold, 2026-10-06. Nothing built yet; numbers below are from a scaling check only.

## The current maze, for comparison

`goalgeo/mazebelief.py:cross_maze`, the maze-belief experiment onwards: 14 cells in a 3 × 11 box.

```
##L###1####       L  = landmark (unique symbol, never noisy)
#aaaa1bbbb3       a/b = symbols 0/1, noisy (eps = 0.4)
##a###2####       1,2,3 = goal cells G1 (centre), G2 (below the right arm), G3 (right end)
```

Start uniform over four corridor cells; passive prefix of up to 2 moves; goal revealed; then H = 5 moves, γ = 0.9;
4 actions (N, S, W, E), a move into a wall leaves the agent in place. Exact belief graph: 4.1 M nodes.

## What sensor noise is

After each move the agent sees only the symbol of the cell it stands on, never its position. With noise eps, the
reading is the cell's own symbol with probability 1 − eps and its sibling (a ↔ b) with probability eps. The landmark
always reads correctly.

Two separate sources of uncertainty about location:

1. **Aliasing**: many cells share a symbol (every left-arm cell shows "a"). Present even with perfect sensors.
2. **Noise**: the reading itself may be wrong, so evidence has to be weighed over several steps rather than used
   to rule cells in or out.

Noise is what makes the exact solver expensive. Without it, the belief is uniform over the cells consistent with
everything seen, and there are few such sets. With it, every reading reweights the probabilities, and the number
of distinct belief vectors multiplies with each step.

## Scaling check

Random 10 × 10 mazes: a perfect maze on a 5 × 5 lattice of rooms (rooms at even coordinates, passages between them),
plus 0–6 extra openings to make loops; about 49–55 open cells; uniform start over all cells; one goal. Distinct
posteriors per move, counted with `goalgeo.mazegraph._step` / `_unique`:

| observations | after 5 / 10 / 15 / 40 moves | exact solver over ~30 moves |
|---|---|---|
| noisy, eps 0.2, 2 symbol pairs, 2 landmarks | 52 k / > 3.5 M by move 8, ~5× per move | not feasible |
| low noise, eps 0.05–0.1, 3 pairs, 3 landmarks | 16 k / 0.5 M / 2.8–3.9 M, still growing | borderline (tens of GB) |
| **noiseless, 2 aliased symbols + 2 landmarks** | 2.8 k / 58 k / 121 k / **159 k (saturates)** | **~14 M nodes for 3 goals × 30 moves: feasible** |
| noiseless, 4 symbols + 2 landmarks | ~890 (localises too fast) | trivial, but the belief is uninteresting |

Crossing a 10 × 10 maze takes ~20–30 moves, so the horizon must be at least that.

## Options

| option | keeps exact Q* / optimal actions | cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Noiseless aliasing** (2 symbols + 2 landmarks, ~55 cells, H ≈ 30) | yes (~14 M nodes) | uncertainty from aliasing only; belief = uniform over a consistent set |
| B. Low noise, short horizon (eps ≈ 0.05, H ≈ 10–12) | yes | the agent cannot cross the maze; goals must be close |
| C. Noisy, approximate solver (eps 0.2; exact filter, QMDP or point-based values) | no | loses the exact ground truth every measure in this project uses |

The exact filter itself is cheap in every option (one vector of ~55 numbers per step); only the solver is limited.
If chosen, A would also need the graph builder to store beliefs sparsely or as cell sets, and longer token sequences
for the transformer (H ≈ 30 instead of 5).
